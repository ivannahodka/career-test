How to Become a Marketer: A Guide for Teens

A marketer sits at the intersection of creativity and analytics: coming up with how to tell people about a product, then checking the numbers to see if it worked. It's one of the most accessible business careers to break into — no degree, no formal education required, but with Google and HubSpot certificates you can earn for free right now.

TL;DR: A marketer promotes products and brands, attracts customers, and builds a growth strategy, working at the intersection of creativity and numbers. The starting point is free Google Ads certification and inbound marketing basics, and the path to a first internship takes 2-4 months.

A marketer is someone who figures out how to get a product or brand noticed by the right people — through ads, social media, email, content, or a mix of all of them. Nearly every company that sells anything needs one, from a small local brand to a global corporation. That's exactly why marketing remains one of the most in-demand careers in business.

What a marketer does

A marketer rarely "just comes up with ads" — the real job is more complex and depends on numbers just as much as ideas:

  • Analyzes the market and audience — studies who buys the product, what matters to them, and what competitors are doing.
  • Builds a promotion strategy — decides which channels to use: Google and Meta ads, social media, email, content marketing, or a combination.
  • Launches and runs campaigns — sets up ads, writes copy, works with designers on visuals.
  • Measures results — checks Google Analytics and ad dashboards to see if a campaign worked, and adjusts strategy based on numbers instead of guesses.

Over time, a marketer becomes the person in the company who best understands the customer and what influences them — a direct path toward brand management, product marketing, or leading a marketing team.

What you need to learn

1. Online marketing and advertising basics

How Google Ads and Meta Ads accounts work, how search ads differ from targeted social ads, how email marketing and SEO work — this is the foundation any campaign is planned from.

2. Basic analytics

The ability to read a Google Analytics report and calculate conversion rate and customer acquisition cost in Excel or Google Sheets — marketing without numbers turns into guessing, not a profession.

3. Writing and pitching ideas

The ability to state clearly and convincingly why a product is worth buying, and to defend your idea in front of a team — a skill that even a great strategy can't succeed without.

Where to start right now

Get Google Ads certified — Google Skillshop — free official training and certification in Google Ads, Google Analytics, and other Google tools.
Take a course on online marketing basics — HubSpot Academy: Inbound Marketing — a free course with a certificate covering content marketing, email, and promotion strategy.
Learn analytics — Google Analytics (Skillshop) — a free course on measuring campaign performance and reading reports.
Build and promote your own project — start a blog, a school page, or a small project's social account and try to grow its first audience — it's the best portfolio piece to start with.

Free resources

  • Google Skillshop — free official courses and certificates in Google Ads, Google Analytics, and other tools
  • HubSpot Academy: Inbound Marketing — free course and certificate on content marketing and promotion strategy
  • HubSpot Marketing Blog — free articles and guides on current marketing topics
  • Neil Patel Blog — free material on SEO, content marketing, and analytics from a well-known practitioner
Your own project is the best start. You don't have to wait for a first job to start practicing: a school page, a blog, or a social account you grow yourself already gives you real numbers for a portfolio — views, followers, engagement.

How much they earn

Marketers are needed by almost any business that sells something — from a small local brand to a large company:

  • Junior Marketer / SMM — entry level, often a first internship with little experience, around $700/month.
  • Middle Marketer — pay grows with experience running independent campaigns and managing ad budgets.
  • Senior / Head of Marketing — leads strategy and a team, pay is noticeably higher, up to $3000/month or more depending on market and company.

The range depends heavily on country, industry, and whether you work in-house or freelance/on your own projects — but the entry bar remains one of the lowest among business careers.

Marketing is for you if...

  • You're equally interested in coming up with ideas and testing them with numbers
  • You like observing people and understanding what shapes their decisions
  • You enjoy writing copy, making content, or putting together ad materials
  • You want to get into a business career quickly, without years of study, and pick a narrower specialty later

It's probably not for you if constantly switching tasks and a fast pace feels draining — in that case, look at "deeper" tracks like analytics or finance.

Frequently asked questions

How is a marketer different from an SMM specialist or ads specialist?

An SMM specialist or ads specialist owns a single channel — social media or an ad account. A marketer thinks bigger: builds the overall promotion strategy, decides which channels to use and in what order, and owns the result as a whole. SMM and paid ads are often the first specialization on the way to a marketer role.

Do I need to know how to code?

No, coding isn't required. Other skills matter more: writing and pitching ideas, basic analytics in Excel/Google Sheets, understanding ad tools like Google Ads and Meta Ads, and the ability to read campaign numbers instead of just "being creative".

Is marketing really one of the most accessible business careers to break into?

Yes. Marketing is one of the few business careers where you can earn Google and HubSpot certificates for free with no prior experience, and your first portfolio piece can be promoting your own school project or blog. That's exactly why it's such a popular entry point into business careers.

Can I start at 13-14 years old?

Yes. You can start learning the basics of marketing, advertising, and analytics for free right now through open courses — real hands-on experience with actual ad budgets will come later once you're old enough for a part-time job or internship.
