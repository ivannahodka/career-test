How to Become a Financier: A Guide for Teens

A financier keeps track of a company's money: how much it earns, how much it spends, and where the next dollar should go. It's one of the most stable business careers — every company, from a small startup to a global corporation, needs financial logic.

TL;DR: Financiers manage a company's money, build budgets and financial models, assess risk, and support investment decisions. You start with solid Excel skills and basic financial formulas — modeling and investment analysis come next.

A financier, broadly, is any specialist who works with a business's financial health: a financial analyst, a budgeting specialist, a future CFO (Chief Financial Officer). They build models that show what will happen to a company's money over the next quarter or year, and help leadership decide where to invest and what to pass on.

What a financier actually does

A financier's work is a constant engagement with numbers — but not mechanical counting, more analysis and forecasting:

  • Builds financial models — creates Excel spreadsheets that show how a company's profit changes under different scenarios.
  • Prepares budgets — plans how much money each department will need next quarter or year.
  • Analyzes investments — evaluates whether a company should invest in a new project, and how long it would take to pay off.
  • Prepares management reporting — turns raw numbers into clear charts and takeaways for leadership.

Financiers talk a lot with leaders of other departments — they need to understand not just the numbers, but the business behind them, so they can explain what those numbers actually mean in practice.

What to learn

1. Confident Excel skills

Financial functions, pivot tables, building models — Excel remains the primary working tool for financiers at nearly every company, regardless of size.

2. Basics of financial modeling

What a DCF model (discounted cash flow) is, how to value a company or a project, how to forecast future revenue based on past data.

3. Financial statements

How to read a P&L (profit and loss statement), a balance sheet, and a cash flow statement — without this, it's impossible to understand a company's real financial position.

How much financiers earn

A financier's salary grows noticeably with experience and specialization:

  • Junior financial analyst — $900 to $1,500/month: basic work with reports and models under a mentor's guidance.
  • Mid-level financier — $1,500 to $2,500/month: independently building models and evaluating investment projects.
  • Senior / on the CFO track — $2,500 to $4,000/month and up: strategic financial decisions and direct work with company leadership.

The range varies a lot by country, company size, and industry — but financial expertise is in demand at nearly every business, which makes this one of the most stable career paths on the job market.

A career in finance might be for you if...

  • You enjoy working with numbers and finding patterns in them
  • You value precision and accuracy — a calculation error can cost a company real money
  • You're interested not just in counting, but in understanding what's behind the numbers — the real business and its decisions
  • You're comfortable working methodically, seeing calculations through to the end instead of quitting halfway

It's probably not for you if you value creative freedom and constantly new tasks without a strict structure — in that case, look at marketing or entrepreneurship instead.

Frequently asked questions

How is a financier different from an accountant?

An accountant records what has already happened to a company's money — keeping the books and preparing tax filings. A financier looks forward: building models, forecasting, and helping decide where the company should invest next.

Do you need advanced math skills?

You need a solid foundation — percentages, ratios, working with formulas in Excel — but not olympiad-level math. Accuracy in calculations and the ability to explain numbers in plain language matter far more.

What's the best way to start at 14–16?

Start with the simplest thing: build your own personal budget in Excel — income, expenses, savings toward a specific goal. It's the same skill financiers use at companies, just at a smaller scale.

Do you need a finance degree?

A finance or economics degree helps a lot when applying to large companies, but the core skills — Excel, financial modeling — can be learned on your own from free materials even before university, giving you a head start in your first year.
