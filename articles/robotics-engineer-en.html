How to Become a Robotics Engineer: Degrees, Skills, First Steps

Updated: November 28, 2026

A robotics engineer designs and builds robots and automated systems — from industrial robot arms in factories to delivery robots, drones and surgical robots. The job sits where three fields meet: mechanical engineering, electronics and programming. Here's what robotics engineers do, which degrees lead there in the US, UK and India, how long it takes and how to start in high school.

In short: most robotics engineers have a bachelor's degree in robotics, mechatronics, mechanical, electrical or computer engineering — usually 4 years in the US and India and 3–4 years in the UK. Math and physics are the key subjects, and programming helps a lot. You can start in high school: Arduino projects, robotics clubs and competitions give you real experience before university.

Robotics is one of the few engineering fields where a teenager can build a working project in a few weeks. That's why many robotics engineers start with a hobby: first an Arduino car, then competitions, then a robotics or mechatronics degree.

What a robotics engineer does

  • Designs the mechanics — the body, actuators, joints, wheels or robot arm.
  • Chooses the electronics — motors, sensors, cameras, controllers and power.
  • Writes code — teaching the robot to move, see, navigate and make decisions.
  • Tests and debugs — finds out why the robot doesn't behave as planned and fixes it.
  • Deploys robots — sets them up in factories and warehouses and trains staff.
  • Works in a team — with mechanical, electrical and software engineers and clients.

Few universities offer a degree called "robotics" — many robotics engineers studied mechanical, electrical or computer engineering and specialized later. Check each university's entry requirements.

Your portfolio matters more than you think. Employers in robotics love to see real projects: a robot you built, a video of it working, code on GitHub, competition results. You can start building this portfolio in high school.

What you need to know

  • Math and physics. You need them to calculate motion, forces and control.
  • Programming. Mostly C/C++ and Python, plus ROS (Robot Operating System).
  • Electronics. Understanding circuits, sensors and motors, and being able to solder.
  • 3D modeling. Designing parts in CAD and 3D-printing prototypes.
  • Systems thinking. A robot is a system where mechanics, electronics and code affect each other.
  • Patience. Robots rarely work the first time — debugging is a big part of the job.

How to start right now

Build your first Arduino project. Start with a blinking LED, then a car that avoids obstacles. No board yet? Try the simulator in Tinkercad Circuits in your browser.
Learn Python or C++. They're the main languages in robotics. A basic level is enough to control your own projects.
Join a robotics club. You'll get equipment, mentors and a team.
Enter competitions. FIRST Robotics and the World Robot Olympiad run in many countries. They give you experience, motivation and a portfolio highlight.

Free resources

  • Arduino documentation — tutorials and examples for beginners
  • Tinkercad — free circuit simulator and 3D modeling in the browser
  • ROS documentation and tutorials — the main software platform for robots
  • MIT OpenCourseWare — free MIT courses, including robotics and control

How much robotics engineers earn

Pay depends on country, industry, experience and skills:

  • Entry-level roles often start as technicians or automation engineers on a solid salary.
  • With experience pay grows noticeably, especially for engineers who combine hardware and software skills.
  • Higher-paying areas often include tech companies, automotive, aerospace and large automated manufacturing.
  • Computer vision and AI skills are especially valuable and can raise pay significantly.

For real figures, check official sources such as the US Bureau of Labor Statistics or salary surveys from professional engineering institutions.

Robotics may be for you if...

  • You love building, taking apart and inventing devices
  • Math and physics go reasonably well for you
  • You enjoy both coding and working with your hands
  • You don't give up when something doesn't work the first time
  • AI, drones and future tech excite you

It's probably not for you if you only want to write code and never touch hardware. Then look at backend development or becoming an ML engineer.

FAQ

How many years does it take to become a robotics engineer?

Usually 4 years for a bachelor's degree in the US and India, and 3–4 years in the UK. Many robotics engineers add a 1–2-year master's degree, especially for research, computer vision or AI roles.

What should I study to become a robotics engineer?

A degree in robotics, mechatronics, mechanical, electrical or computer engineering. In high school focus on math, physics and programming. Check each university's exact entry requirements.

At what age can I start learning robotics?

Simple kits work for kids aged 7–10, and from about 12–14 you can build Arduino projects and enter competitions. The earlier you start, the more experience and projects you'll have when applying to university.

Will AI replace robotics engineers?

No — if anything, AI increases demand for people who can put it into real machines. AI helps write code and analyze data, but engineers still need to build, tune and take responsibility for robots in the real world.
