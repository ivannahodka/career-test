How to Become an HR Manager: A Guide for Teens

An HR manager connects people and companies: finding the right specialists, helping them settle into a team, and growing alongside them. This career is built on understanding people rather than numbers or code — a great fit for anyone more interested in conversation and motivation than sitting behind calculations.

TL;DR: HR managers find talent, develop people, and build company culture. You start with basic communication and interviewing skills — working with ATS systems, onboarding, and HR strategy come next.

HR (human resources) is the function responsible for everything related to people at a company: from finding and hiring new employees to training them, developing them, and building a comfortable work environment. Within HR there are different roles — recruiter (finds and hires people), HR business partner (works with teams), training specialist — but they all share one thing: the work revolves around people, not a product or numbers.

What an HR manager actually does

An HR manager's work changes day to day, but usually revolves around a few recurring tasks:

  • Sources candidates — reviews resumes and looks for the right people for open roles through LinkedIn and other platforms.
  • Conducts interviews — assesses not just a candidate's skills, but whether they'll fit the team and company culture.
  • Handles onboarding — helps new employees settle in quickly: introducing them to the team, processes, and tools.
  • Develops people — organizes training and tracks employees' career growth inside the company.

A good HR manager is equally comfortable talking to a candidate in an interview and to a company leader discussing what kind of people the business needs to grow.

What to learn

1. Recruiting and interviewing basics

How to write a good job posting, where to find candidates, what questions to ask in an interview to assess not just experience but soft skills too — this is the foundation nearly every HR professional starts with.

2. Working with ATS systems

An ATS (Applicant Tracking System) is software for managing applications and candidates, like the tools offered by LinkedIn Recruiter or Workday. Being able to navigate them quickly saves dozens of hours a month.

3. Understanding people and communication

HR constantly deals with emotions and motivation — the ability to listen carefully, ask the right questions, and resolve conflicts between employees matters just as much as formal process knowledge.

Start small. Organize something that requires finding and coordinating people — a school event, a club, a volunteer group. It's the same logic HR uses: figure out who's needed, find the right people, and keep them motivated.

How much HR professionals earn

An HR professional's salary grows with experience and specialization:

  • Junior recruiter — $600 to $1,000/month: sourcing candidates and running initial interviews under a mentor's guidance.
  • Mid-level HR manager — $1,000 to $1,800/month: independently managing open roles, onboarding, and working with teams.
  • Senior HR / HR business partner — $1,800 to $2,500/month and up: building company HR strategy and working directly with leadership.

The range varies a lot by country, industry, and company size — but HR remains one of the few roles needed by absolutely every business, no matter what it does.

HR might be for you if...

  • You're more interested in talking to people and understanding their motivation than working with numbers or code
  • You listen carefully and notice details others miss
  • You're comfortable being a go-between — between a candidate and a company, an employee and a manager
  • You're organized and can juggle several processes at once without losing track of details

It's probably not for you if you'd rather work alone on clearly defined technical tasks without constant interaction with different people — in that case, look at analytics or finance instead.

Frequently asked questions

How is an HR manager different from a recruiter?

A recruiter usually only handles sourcing and hiring candidates. An HR manager is responsible for a broader range of tasks: onboarding, training, employee development, company culture, and HR strategy across the whole company. Recruiting is often the first step on the way to an HR manager role.

Do you need a psychology degree?

Not formally — many successful HR professionals come from other fields. But understanding basic communication and motivation psychology helps a lot: HR work is built around talking to people and reading their behavior.

What's the best way to start at 14–16?

Try organizing something that involves finding and coordinating people — a school event, a volunteer group, a team project. It trains exactly the skills HR needs: finding the right people, assigning roles, and keeping a team motivated.

Can you start without company work experience?

Yes. Many people start as a junior recruiter, where the main job is sourcing candidates for open roles and running initial interviews. It doesn't require prior HR experience, but it does require attentiveness to people and strong organization.
